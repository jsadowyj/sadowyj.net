Once I reach an intermediate level, my routine changes. Two of those changes are already covered elsewhere: I [stop importing whole lessons into Anki]({{< relref "reading-and-anki.md#sending-words-to-anki" >}}), and I let my [tutor sessions decide what goes into Anki]({{< relref "speaking.md#choosing-what-goes-into-anki" >}}) instead. The biggest new piece is listening.

## The listening gap

So much of my study revolves around reading that by the time I reach an intermediate level, my listening comprehension isn't great. I can read a sentence just fine, then completely miss the same sentence when someone says it out loud.

The good news is that this is a pivotal moment in your language learning journey. Your reading comprehension is likely high enough now that you can read subtitles at near-native speed. All you're missing is the right tool.

## Sluka

Enter [Sluka](https://www.sluka.app/). It turns entertainment into a language learning apparatus. It's a browser extension (Chrome, Brave, and Edge) that works on the subtitles of YouTube, Netflix, and a bunch of other video sites.

- **It imports your known words from LingQ,** so you don't start from zero. (Words → Tools → Import from LingQ.)
- **It keeps track of every word you know,** across everything you watch.
- **Here's the kicker: it hides the subtitles for lines you already know.** If you know every word in a line, the subtitle disappears, and you have to understand it by ear. That's what forces your listening comprehension to improve. The setting is called **Hide lines you know**, and it's off by default, so turn it on in the subtitle menu.
- **For lines where you don't know all of the words,** it shows you a translation, and you can track how well you know each word with levels, just like in LingQ.

The first 1,000 words in each language are free, and after that it's a one-time $15.

Spending 30 minutes to an hour every day with this tool is a game changer, and I can't recommend it enough.

## My routine at this level

At this point, Sluka replaces LingQ entirely: both the reading and the listening. My routine becomes:

- **Anki reviews,** with new cards coming from my [tutor sessions]({{< relref "speaking.md#choosing-what-goes-into-anki" >}}) instead of LingQ lessons.
- **30 minutes to an hour of Sluka** every day.
- **A few iTalki lessons a week.**
